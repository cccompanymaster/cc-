    Short answer

    US brands running Meta and Google ads in Korea should treat server-side conversion tracking (Meta Conversions API, Google Enhanced Conversions) as mandatory, use native Korean creative, and split the first budget by how their category is searched and discovered — including Naver. About a third of Korean mobile traffic runs on iOS (StatCounter, mid-2026), so Pixel-only measurement under-counts conversions from that segment.

    Most US brands enter Korea expecting Meta and Google to behave the same way they do at home. They don't. About a third of Korean mobile traffic is iOS, server-side conversion tracking is the baseline rather than an extra, Korean creative needs different casting and copy, and Naver sits alongside Google for search. This is the playbook for running Meta and Google in Korea alongside Naver.

    1. Why Foreign Brands Underperform on Korean Facebook & Google

    Korea's online ad spend reached roughly ₩10.1 trillion in 2024, according to the KMCC/KOBACO broadcasting and communications ad spend survey. There is no official breakdown by platform, but Naver, Google, Meta and Kakao all hold meaningful attention, so no single platform covers Korean consumers on its own.

    The three most expensive mistakes repeat across first launches. The first is importing US creative — even translated, US-shot creative feels foreign in casting, pacing and on-screen text. The second is underestimating iOS measurement loss: iOS carries roughly 34–36% of Korean mobile traffic (StatCounter, mid-2026), so a campaign relying on the Meta Pixel without server-side tracking under-counts conversions from about a third of its audience. The third is treating Naver and Google as the same channel — they aren't; Naver is central to Korean-language intent queries, while Google captures global brand searches and many mobile sessions.

    The pragmatic default is a tri-channel plan: Naver search + Google paid + Meta paid, weighted toward the channel that best matches your category's purchase intent (§6).

    2. Meta Ads in Korea — Measurement and Targeting

    Instagram and Reels carry much of Meta's reach in Korea. Because iOS App Tracking Transparency limits what Meta can attribute from iPhone users, install the Meta Pixel and the Conversions API together and deduplicate events with an event ID; treat a Korean Meta launch without server-side tracking as a measurement gap, and budget engineering time or a managed partner for it.

    In Ads Manager you choose one of six objectives (awareness, traffic, engagement, leads, app promotion, sales). Advantage+ audience and automated shopping campaigns depend on a strong conversion signal, so fix measurement before relying on automation. Costs are set by auction and vary by category, audience, creative and season — there is no reliable public Korean CPM benchmark, so read costs from your own test account.

    Regulated categories (cosmetics, health functional food, medical, finance) also follow Korean advertising rules — see our advertising-law guide for the KFTC disclosure rules and category pre-reviews.

    3. Google Ads in Korea — Search, YouTube, Performance Max

    Search is the highest-intent inventory. CPCs depend on category competition and are set by auction. Do not import a US keyword set directly — Korean searchers add different long-tail suffixes ("어디 좋아요?", "후기", "추천"), so build the keyword list from Korean query research.

    YouTube is the reach pillar. Use Korean voice-over rather than dubbed English, reveal the brand early in skippable in-stream ads, and use short bumpers for awareness pulses rather than bottom-of-funnel goals.

    Performance Max routes spend automatically across Google surfaces, which is efficient when the conversion signal is strong and wasteful when tracking is misconfigured. Set up Enhanced Conversions or offline conversion import and confirm that conversions are flowing before you turn it on.

    App campaigns remain the main tool for apps, though SKAdNetwork limits iOS measurement granularity.

    4. Korean Audience Targeting — Beyond US Pixel Patterns

    The biggest targeting mistake is cloning the US audience structure. The Korean audience graph differs because local platforms (KakaoTalk, Naver, Coupang) sit outside Meta's and Google's data, and because customer data use follows Korea's Personal Information Protection Act. A lookalike trained on US pixel data is a poor predictor of Korean conversion.

    • Custom audiences: uploaded lists need a minimum matched size before they deliver, and Korean customers' email and phone data may match less often than you expect — check the matched size in Ads Manager and upload larger seed lists.
    • Lookalikes: seed from Korean buyers only rather than mixing US and Korean purchasers; for Google, Customer Match from your Cafe24 or Shopify buyers complements in-market segments.
    • Interests that segment cleanly in Korea: Korean cultural anchors — K-pop fandoms, K-drama viewers, webtoon readers, golf, camping (캠핑), home café (홈카페) — combined with a behavioral signal.
    • Off-platform behavior: Naver Pay and Coupang membership data cannot be uploaded to Meta or Google; reflect those audiences in creative and bidding instead.
    • Consent: uploading customer lists is processing personal data — make sure your privacy notice and consents cover it, and follow PIPA's rules for cross-border transfers.

    5. Creative Localization — What to Change First

    Korean voice-over. The highest-leverage day-one change is swapping English voice-over for native Korean audio. Test it against your current creative with the same script and shots.

    Copy density. Machine-translated US headlines read as either sparse or cluttered in Korean. Write Korean headlines natively, keep them to two lines, and shorten further for Reels and Shorts thumbnails.

    Vertical first. Produce 9:16 assets first and treat 16:9 as a desktop fallback.

    Local trust signals. Korean talent in the thumbnail or first frame and KRW (₩) pricing on the creative make the ad feel made for Korea.

    CTA destination. For consideration-stage products where buyers want a question answered first, "카카오톡으로 상담받기" (Chat on KakaoTalk) routed to a KakaoTalk Channel often fits Korean buyers better than a web form; advertising messages sent later through that channel need prior consent.

    6. Budget Allocation — Setting and Testing the First Split

    No universal split. Neither Meta, Google nor Naver publishes Korean budget or ROAS benchmarks, and results depend on margin, average order value, category and measurement. Start from where your category's buyers search and discover, then move budget to what converts.

    Judge on backend revenue. In-platform attribution under-counts iOS conversions even with server-side tracking, so compare channels on your Cafe24, Shopify or NHN Commerce backend. On Google, Enhanced Conversions and a server-side tag setup close much of the gap.

    Size the test. There is no official minimum budget. Give each channel enough budget to produce conversions you can compare, and if the budget is small, test fewer channels well.

    Expect a learning period. Automated bidding needs time and conversion volume to learn Korean audiences. Set evaluation dates and targets before launch so you don't pull spend on early noise.

    FAQ

    How much do Meta and Google ads cost in Korea?

    There is no reliable public benchmark: Meta and Google set costs by auction, so they depend on category, audience, creative and season. Run a small test with server-side conversion tracking (Meta Conversions API, Google Enhanced Conversions) and read costs from your own account before committing budget.

    What ROAS can you expect from Meta, Google and Naver ads in Korea?

    None of the platforms publishes Korean ROAS benchmarks, and ROAS depends on margin, average order value, category and measurement. Compare channels on backend revenue from Cafe24, Shopify or NHN Commerce rather than in-platform attribution alone, because iOS limits mean platform numbers under-count conversions.

    What is the minimum budget to test Meta and Google ads in Korea?

    There is no official minimum. Size the test so each channel you run can produce enough conversions to compare; if the budget is small, test fewer channels — for example Naver search plus one of Meta or Google — rather than spreading it thinly across three.
