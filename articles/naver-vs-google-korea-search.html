    Short answer

    It depends on which tracker you read. InternetTrend, which compiles traffic logs from Korean websites, put Naver at 64.28% and Google at 28.37% for January–June 2026; StatCounter put Google at 49.54% and Naver at 40.9% in July 2026. Either way, Naver wins Korean commerce and local intent, while Google is strongest for developer, English-language, video, academic and B2B software queries.

    Ask who leads Korean search and you get two answers. Korea's domestic tracker shows Naver far ahead; StatCounter's global panel shows Google slightly ahead. For US brands deciding where to invest SEO budget in Korea, the useful question in 2026 is not "Naver or Google?" but "which engine for which intent, and how do AI answers change discovery?" This article lays out both share measures, where each engine wins, the SEO tactics that matter on each, and how Naver's AI Briefing, Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, and ChatGPT search are reshaping discovery.

    1. Korean Search Market Share 2026 — Two Trackers, Two Leaders

    The two most-cited trackers disagree because they count different things. InternetTrend compiles traffic logs from Korean websites and put Naver at 64.28% and Google at 28.37% for the first half of 2026, with Naver up 2.46 points on the previous half-year. StatCounter counts search referrals to the sites that carry its tracking code; for July 2026 it showed Google at 49.54%, Naver at 40.9% and Bing at 5.44%. Neither is "the" market share. If you quote one, name the tracker and the period.

    The same caution applies to the device and age splits that circulate in Korean marketing decks: few are published with a method. If you need a split, use a source that publishes its method. StatCounter, for example, lets you filter by platform.

    The other surface foreign brands underestimate is YouTube as a search engine. Many Korean shoppers start product research with creator reviews on YouTube. YouTube is technically Google's surface, but its SEO requirements (Korean titles, Korean voice-over, retention) are entirely separate from Google web search. Treating YouTube as a third search engine matches how Korean users actually behave.

    2. Why Naver Still Wins for Korean Commerce Intent

    Naver's structural moat is not its crawler; it's the fact that Naver owns the answer surfaces. When a Korean user searches "강남 피부과 추천" (recommend a Gangnam dermatologist), Google returns web links plus a Maps box. Naver returns a stack of Smart Block modules: Place listings with booking buttons, blog and Cafe posts, Knowledge iN answers and more. The user can find an answer without leaving Naver, and a generation of Korean searchers has come to expect search to work that way.

    Smart Block (스마트블록) is Naver's modular results layout. In February 2024 Naver replaced its VIEW tab with a popular-posts (인기글) block and split Blog and Cafe into separate tabs. Which blocks appear, and in what order, changes with the query, so check the blocks your target queries actually show before you plan content. Foreign brands that win on Naver usually appear in several blocks at once, through Power Link ads, disclosed influencer reviews, organic blog content and a verified Naver Place or Smartstore listing.

    Naver Place (네이버 플레이스) is the most under-used surface for foreign brands with any physical presence. For local queries (clinics, restaurants, cafés, fitness studios), Place listings sit at the top of the mobile results. Naver says Place results vary with the query, the searcher's location and context, and are updated often. What you control is a complete listing: Korean address and business name, a verified phone number, photos and real reviews.

    Naver Cafe (네이버 카페) is the unsung commerce engine. Korean buyers, especially in beauty, parenting, supplements and outdoor/camping, read Cafe communities for real-user reviews before buying. The path forward is not paid posting disguised as members, which breaks the KFTC's endorsement rules and Cafe terms. Ask real customers to share their experience instead. If you offer them anything in return, the post must say so in its title or opening.

    Knowledge iN (지식iN) remains useful for long-tail informational queries, and Naver marks answers from verified professionals (doctors, pharmacists, lawyers and others) as expert answers. For regulated categories, an expert answer that promotes a business is still advertising. Clinic content, for example, must follow the Medical Service Act.

    The cumulative effect is that on commerce queries, Naver feels less like a search engine and more like a content marketplace where the brand present on the most surfaces wins. Google's "best page wins" logic does not carry over.

    3. Where Google Wins in Korea — Five Query Types

    Foreign brands often hear "Naver wins Korea" and conclude Google is irrelevant. That's wrong. Google is strongest on five kinds of queries:

    1. Developer and technical queries. Korean software engineers lean on Google for Stack Overflow, GitHub, framework docs and the Korean dev blogs that index well there. Naver's developer content is healthy but rarely outranks Google for English technical terms.
    2. English-language and global content. When Korean users research a US company, an international news event or a global brand without a Korean presence, they often search Google in English, even mid-session in Korean. For cross-border B2B brands, your English SEO reaches Korean buyers before they ever search in Korean.
    3. YouTube video search. How-to and product-demo intent runs through YouTube, which is Google's surface. Naver's video results don't replace it.
    4. Academic and research queries. Google Scholar, university repositories and English-language journals dominate here. RISS and DBpia cover Korean theses, but researchers cross-check on Google.
    5. B2B software reviews. Korean SaaS buyers read G2, Capterra and analyst reviews, which rank on Google, not Naver. For B2B SaaS brands entering Korea, Google can be the higher-return search channel even though Naver leads overall on the domestic tracker.

    The strategic implication is that Google in Korea is not a general-purpose backup to Naver. It is the specialist engine for high-intent informational and English-leaning queries. Map your target keywords by query type before you set the budget split. Don't set it from a headline share figure.

    4. How Korean Users Actually Search — Multi-Engine Sessions

    Many Korean users don't pick one engine. A single purchase decision often runs across YouTube, Naver and Google, and increasingly an AI assistant. Treating Korean search as a single-engine choice is the foundational mistake foreign brands make. Four patterns come up again and again:

    • The Naver-first commerce loop. For commerce intent (cosmetics, restaurants, clinics, supplements), users scroll Smart Block, read blog posts or Cafe threads, then buy on Naver Shopping/Smartstore or compare prices on Coupang and 11st. Google enters late, if at all, usually to check English-language reviews of imported brands.
    • The Google-first information loop. For "how does X work," "what is Y," developer questions and anything English-leaning, users start on Google and fall back to Naver when Google's Korean results are thin.
    • The YouTube-first product loop. For tech, beauty and fashion, a creator review on YouTube often starts the journey. Users then read text reviews and compare prices on Naver, and buy on Coupang or Naver Shopping. YouTube works as the awareness funnel and Naver/Coupang as the conversion funnel.
    • The KakaoTalk-first local loop. For local services, some users skip search and ask in a KakaoTalk group chat or a community Cafe. Traditional SEO tracking can't see this. Brands that are easy to recommend in chat (a clear Korean name, a KakaoTalk channel, an easy referral path) still capture it.

    For SEO planning, foreign brands need a parallel-track strategy: Naver for Korea-specific commerce surfaces, Google for English and informational surfaces, YouTube for awareness video, and a KakaoTalk channel for the conversation after search. Optimizing one engine and expecting it to carry the whole funnel rarely works.

    5. SEO Tactics That Work — Naver Smart Block vs Google E-E-A-T

    Naver SEO is surface SEO. The goal is not to "rank #1 on Naver," because a Smart Block results page has no single #1. The goal is to occupy as many blocks as possible for your target query: blog, Place, Shopping, influencer reviews and Knowledge iN. Each block ranks on its own. Naver has described C-Rank as a measure of how credible a source is on a topic and D.I.A. as a model that rates documents for first-hand experience and information, so consistent, specialized posting on Naver Blog matters more than generic web links.

    The Naver playbook has six steps:

    1. Register a verified Naver Blog for the brand and post regularly in one specialty, with your own photos and first-hand detail.
    2. Work with Naver influencers on sponsored reviews that disclose the sponsorship in the title or opening, as the KFTC's December 2024 guideline requires.
    3. Keep a complete Naver Place listing if you have any Korean location.
    4. Build Cafe conversations through real customer relationships rather than astroturfing.
    5. Answer Knowledge iN questions through credentialed Korean experts, within advertising law.
    6. Register your site in Naver Search Advisor and submit a sitemap.

    Google SEO in Korea is global SEO with a Korean accent. The helpful-content and E-E-A-T principles that matter in the US apply almost identically: author bios with credentials, original primary research, citations, structured data and fast pages. The Korean-specific layers are hreflang ko + en pairing, Korean-language entities tied to the brand, Korean reviews on Google Business Profile, and deep Korean voice-of-customer content (FAQs, support docs).

    The biggest tactical mistake we see is a single-track program. Pure Naver misses Google's high-intent informational surface; pure Google misses Naver, which leads on Korea's domestic tracker. The right structure is two parallel SEO programs that share one content brief, run by specialists who understand each engine's signals.

    6. The 2026 AI Search Shift — AI Briefing, AI Overviews, ChatGPT Search

    The biggest unknown for Korean SEO in 2026 is how fast AI answers eat into traditional search sessions. No official tracker measures AI search's share of Korean queries. The closest official data point is usage: in the 2025 Intelligent Information Society User Panel Survey, published in May 2026 by the Korea Media and Communications Commission and KISDI, 38.9% of Koreans aged 18–72 had used generative AI, up from 24.0% a year earlier (that survey covered ages 15–69). The most common reason was more efficient information search (86.0%).

    Naver has put generative answers into its own results. AI Briefing has summarized answers in Naver search for all users since March 2025, and it uses Naver's own content (blog, Cafe and Clip posts) as its sources. In March 2026 Naver said it would close Cue and Clova X and fold that work into a new AI tab. Content that already ranks in Naver's blocks is the content AI Briefing can cite. Brands strong on Naver SEO keep their advantage, and brands that ignored Naver start behind.

    ChatGPT search and Perplexity answer from the open web. A well-structured Korean version of your site, with hreflang, Korean-localized author bios and clear FAQs, can be cited even where it doesn't rank on Naver. Google offers AI Overviews in Korea and has served AI Mode in Korean since September 9, 2025. The fundamentals are the same as classic Google SEO: an answer-first structure, FAQ markup, named authors and pages Google can crawl.

    Measure it rather than guess. ChatGPT referrals arrive tagged utm_source=chatgpt.com in GA4, and Google Search Console added a generative AI performance report in June 2026.

    The hedge strategy. Lead with Naver for commerce and local queries and with Google for developer, English-language and B2B queries. Build AI-search readiness into both: clear FAQ schema, definitive answer paragraphs, named-author pages, original primary data and proper hreflang. AI search rewards clarity over keyword stuffing, and the brands building this discipline now will compound it as AI's share grows.
