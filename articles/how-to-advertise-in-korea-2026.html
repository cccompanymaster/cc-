    Short answer

    Foreign brands advertising in Korea should run native Korean creative across the platforms Korean consumers actually use — Naver, Kakao, Meta, Google/YouTube and, for brand building, Korean CTV — and clear Korean disclosure and category rules before launch. Korea's total ad spend was ₩17.27 trillion in 2025, with online taking about 60%, according to KOBACO.

    Advertising in Korea is not "running US Meta ads with Korean copy." Korea has its own ad market, local platforms (Naver, Kakao) that don't exist anywhere else, and advertising rules enforced by Korean regulators (KFTC, MFDS, KMCC). This guide walks foreign brands through the market, the platforms to plan for, compliance, creative requirements, and a 90-day launch playbook.

    1. The Korean Ad Market — Size and Where the Money Flows

    According to KOBACO's review of the 2025 ad market, Korea's total ad spend was about ₩17.27 trillion, roughly ₩140 billion more than the year before. Online advertising accounted for about 60% of the total, while broadcast advertising fell by about 13% year on year. The growth bet for foreign brands is digital; traditional TV is a smaller and shrinking slice.

    Platform shares. There is no official breakdown of digital ad spend by company, so this guide does not quote platform shares. What matters for a foreign brand is that Korean consumer attention sits on Naver and Kakao as well as Google and Meta — budgets planned only around Google and Meta miss a large part of Korean search and messaging behavior.

    2. The Five Ad Platforms to Plan For — Naver, Kakao, Meta, Google, Korean CTV

    1) Naver Ads. Naver sells search ads (Power Link), brand search placements and display/native ads. In March 2026 Naver introduced a new AI-enhanced ad platform that brings search and display buying together, so menu names may differ from older guides. Check the current account requirements for foreign advertisers in Naver's ad system, or work through an agency.

    2) Google + YouTube. The same Google Ads account that runs your US campaigns can target Korea, but Korean keyword sets, YouTube creative localization and Performance Max learning need separate treatment. If you hire an agency, its Google Partner badge links to a Google-hosted profile you can verify.

    3) Kakao Moment + KakaoTalk Channel. Kakao Moment is the buying platform for the Kakao ecosystem, including the Bizboard banner inside the KakaoTalk chat list and display placements. KakaoTalk had 48.93 million domestic monthly active users in Q2 2024, according to Kakao. A KakaoTalk Channel is your owned-audience CRM; advertising messages require the recipient's prior consent under the Network Act (Article 50).

    4) Meta — Facebook + Instagram + Reels. In Ads Manager you pick one of six objectives (awareness, traffic, engagement, leads, app promotion, sales). Install the Meta Pixel and the Conversions API together and deduplicate events with an event ID.

    5) Korean CTV — TVING, Wavve, Coupang Play. Premium streaming video suits brand-building, new-product launches and category creation rather than direct response. Check each service's ad products and review rules before booking.

    Other surfaces. Daangn (Karrot) offers hyperlocal ads with neighborhood and radius targeting; TikTok reaches younger audiences; Naver Cafe communities matter in parenting, beauty and outdoor categories — but any sponsored community content must follow each cafe's rules and carry a clear ad disclosure.

    3. Compliance Must-Knows — KFTC, MFDS, KMCC for Foreign Advertisers

    KFTC (Korea Fair Trade Commission) regulates advertising under the Act on Fair Labeling and Advertising and its endorsement guidelines. Since December 1, 2024, text posts such as blogs must disclose an economic relationship in the title or opening, photo posts inside the image or on the first line, and videos in the title or at the start and end; a disclosure hidden among many hashtags or behind "more" is unlikely to count. Since June 1, 2026, ads that use AI-generated virtual people must disclose that they are virtual.

    MFDS (식품의약품안전처) administers the product laws for cosmetics, health functional foods, food and medical devices. Cosmetics may not be advertised in ways that make them look like medicines, and health functional food advertising goes through a review system before it runs. Confirm category-specific rules before you produce creative.

    KMCC (방송미디어통신위원회), which replaced the Korea Communications Commission in October 2025, oversees broadcasting and communications. Digital-first brands mostly meet its rules once they expand into broadcast-style video.

    Medical advertising falls under the Medical Service Act. Article 56 bans, among other things, patient testimonials that could mislead about treatment effects and comparisons with other providers; violating Article 56(1)–(3) can lead to up to one year in prison or a fine of up to ₩10 million under Article 89. Ads on media designated by the Enforcement Decree must be pre-reviewed before they run.

    The pragmatic compliance stack. Use Korean legal review for launch creative, build a creative review step into every launch, keep dated screenshots of published assets, and require influencers by contract to disclose sponsorship in the required position.

    4. Creative Requirements — What Korean Ads Actually Look Like

    Korean ad creative differs from US creative in five ways foreign brands consistently underestimate.

    1) Korean voice and copy — native, not translated. Translated headlines often read too sparse or too cluttered. Hire a Korean copywriter, not a translator, and use native Korean voice-over for video.

    2) Mobile-first, vertical by default. Produce 9:16 vertical assets first and treat 16:9 desktop as a fallback. Keep Korean headlines on Reels and Shorts short enough to read in two lines.

    3) KRW pricing on the creative. Show prices in won (₩) on the ad and the landing page; USD prices make Korean buyers think about exchange rates and import costs.

    4) Local trust signals. Korean talent, Korean settings and familiar local context (KakaoTalk chat UI, Korean street scenes) make an ad feel made for Korea.

    5) KakaoTalk-routed CTAs. For consideration-stage products, a "카카오톡으로 상담받기 (Chat on KakaoTalk)" button routed to your KakaoTalk Channel meets Korean buyers where they already message.

    5. The 90-Day Launch Playbook — Week-by-Week

    Weeks 1–4 — Foundation. Set up ad accounts that can actually buy Korean media (directly or through an agency whose platform status you have verified); install the Meta Pixel and Conversions API; configure Google Ads and GA4; create a KakaoTalk Channel and Naver Place listing. If you process Korean users' personal data, designate a privacy officer (PIPA Article 31) and, if you have no Korean address and meet the decree thresholds, a domestic representative (Article 31-2).

    Weeks 5–8 — Creative production and compliance checks. Produce several native Korean variants (video, static, KakaoTalk Channel banners) with Korean voice-over and KRW pricing. Check endorsement disclosures and category rules (medical pre-review, health functional food review, cosmetics claims). Build Korean landing pages with a KakaoTalk Channel CTA, KRW pricing, tax-invoice (세금계산서) information, and a Korean privacy policy and terms.

    Weeks 9–12 — Soft launch. Go live on Naver search and display, Google Search and Performance Max, Meta prospecting and retargeting, and Kakao Bizboard with a test budget. Add YouTube and CTV once you have a baseline conversion signal. Review daily at first, then weekly.

    Week 13 — Scale-or-stop decision. Review ROAS, CPA and qualified-lead volume against pre-launch targets. Three verdicts: scale the winning channels and creative; keep spend flat while iterating; or pause and diagnose before relaunching. Set the targets before launch so the decision is not made on noise.

    6. Budget Allocation by Industry — Where to Put the Weight

    There is no official benchmark for how foreign brands should split budgets in Korea. Start from where your category's buyers search and talk, test, then move budget to what converts.

    Beauty and K-beauty. Naver search and shopping plus Instagram creators carry discovery; every paid creator post needs a disclosure in the required position.

    Fashion and lifestyle. Meta, Instagram and Reels drive awareness and creator-led discovery; Naver supports comparison and purchase.

    Health and supplements. Korean consumers research health products on Naver search and communities. Check MFDS category rules first — they shape what the creative can say.

    Travel and hotel. Google search plus Naver's domestic-travel surfaces; Meta drives inspiration.

    B2B / SaaS. Google Search and LinkedIn lead; Naver supports Korean-language category queries. Allow more time for conversion signals because B2B sales cycles are long.

    Size the test, not a rule of thumb. Give each channel enough budget to produce conversions you can compare. If the budget is small, run fewer channels well rather than five channels thinly.

    FAQ

    How much budget do you need to test advertising in Korea?

    There is no official minimum. Size the test so each channel you run can produce enough conversions to compare; if the budget is small, start with one or two channels, such as Naver search plus Meta, rather than spreading it across five platforms.

    How long does it take to launch ads in Korea?

    Plan roughly a quarter: foundation work (ad accounts, measurement, KakaoTalk Channel, privacy roles), creative production and compliance checks, a soft launch, and a scale-or-stop review against targets set before launch. Account verification, category ad rules and creative production drive the timeline.

    What ad disclosure and pre-approval rules apply to foreign brands in Korea?

    Under the KFTC endorsement guidelines, since December 1, 2024 paid text posts must disclose the relationship in the title or opening and videos in the title or at the start and end; a disclosure buried among hashtags or behind "more" is unlikely to count. Medical ads fall under Medical Service Act Article 56 and need pre-review on media designated by decree, health functional food ads go through a review system, and cosmetics may not be advertised as if they were medicines.
