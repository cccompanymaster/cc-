    Short answer

    A US brand entering Korea needs Naver SEO, Google SEO, a KakaoTalk channel and a Coupang or Smartstore listing, all in fluent Korean. Search share depends on the tracker: InternetTrend, which compiles traffic logs from Korean websites, put Naver at 64.28% and Google at 28.37% for January–June 2026; StatCounter put Google at 49.54% and Naver at 40.9% in July 2026. Either way, both engines matter.

    If you are bringing a US brand into Korea, the playbook you used in Google, Meta and Amazon does not transfer one-to-one. Korea runs on Naver, Kakao and Coupang, and the local algorithm rewards entirely different signals. This article maps both search-share trackers, the .kr domain choices, and a step-by-step 90-day Korean launch checklist.

    1. Why Korea Is Different — Naver vs Google Share

    Korean search splits mainly between Naver and Google, and the two most-cited trackers disagree on who leads. InternetTrend, which compiles traffic logs from Korean websites, put Naver at 64.28% and Google at 28.37% for January–June 2026. StatCounter, which counts search referrals to the sites carrying its tracking code, showed Google at 49.54% and Naver at 40.9% in July 2026. The implication for foreign brands is twofold: you cannot win Korean SEO with English-only Google tactics, and you cannot ignore Google either.

    Beyond search, KakaoTalk is Korea's default messenger, and Coupang and Naver Shopping lead online retail. A serious Korea entry plan therefore covers Naver SEO + Google SEO + KakaoTalk channel + Coupang or Smartstore listing, all in fluent Korean.

    2. Step 1 — Local Entity, Domain, and Hosting

    The first decision is whether you need a local entity. Regulated sectors (medical, financial, telecom) come with licensing rules that usually require one. Many consumer brands start with the US entity plus a .kr or co.kr domain. Host close to Korean users, through a Seoul cloud region or a CDN with Korean edge locations, so Korean pages load fast on mobile.

    If you set up a Korean business to sell directly, file an online-retailer report (통신판매업 신고) under Article 12 of the E-Commerce Act. Integrate a Korean payment gateway such as NICE, KG INICIS or Toss Payments, and offer the local wallets Korean shoppers expect, such as Naver Pay and KakaoPay.

    3. Step 2 — Naver SEO Basics for Foreign Brands

    Naver's organic surface is structured around Smart Block, which groups results by intent (place, product, blog, knowledge, image). Since February 2024, blog and Cafe content appears in a popular-posts block, and Blog and Cafe have separate tabs. Three rules separate winning Korean pages from invisible ones:

    • Original Korean content on a verified blog (preferably Naver Blog), posted regularly in one specialty with your own photos and first-hand detail. Naver has described C-Rank (source credibility on a topic) and D.I.A. (document quality, including experience) as core signals.
    • Brand entity signals: a complete Naver Place listing if you have a Korean location, a verified Naver Blog channel and consistent Korean brand naming.
    • Links and mentions from relevant Korean sites, such as press, partners and industry associations.

    Foreign brands often get stuck because their global site sits on .com in English. Add a Korean subfolder (/ko/) with properly localized content. Then register the site in Naver Search Advisor and submit a sitemap so Naver can find the Korean pages.

    4. Step 3 — Localization Beyond Translation

    Word-for-word translation is the most common Korea launch mistake. Korean buyers expect brand stories told in their own cultural references: seasonal greetings (Chuseok, Lunar New Year), family contexts and familiar visual cues. We rebuild landing pages with Korean models, KRW pricing and a KakaoTalk channel as the primary CTA, replacing the usual "Email us" button.

    Reviews matter more than ads, and Korean buyers read them closely. Plan a structured Korean reviewer (체험단) campaign in the first 90 days. Each post must disclose the sponsorship in its title or opening, as the KFTC's December 2024 guideline requires.

    5. Step 4 — Channel Mix for a Korean Launch

    6. Common Mistakes & 90-Day Launch Checklist

    The five most expensive mistakes we see from US brands entering Korea:

    1. Launching with English-only assets and expecting Korean SEO to follow.
    2. Skipping a Naver Place listing when you have a real Korean location (office, showroom or clinic).
    3. Using global influencers instead of Korean creators whose audience matches your category.
    4. Ignoring the legal compliance layer (KFTC endorsement disclosures, medical advertising pre-review).
    5. No Korean phone number or KakaoTalk channel, which kills conversion at the last step.

    Our 90-day checklist condenses these into three blocks. Days 1–30 cover legal setup, the .kr domain, Korean copy and a verified Naver Blog. Days 31–60 focus on indexing, the first reviewer cohort and KakaoTalk channel growth. Days 61–90 unlock paid amplification and the Coupang or Smartstore listing.

    Many consumer brands start with the US entity plus a .kr or co.kr domain, while regulated sectors such as medical, financial and telecom come with licensing rules that usually require a local entity.
