    Short answer

    US brands should launch on one primary platform, Coupang or Naver Smartstore, and add the other once the first is stable: Coupang first when fast delivery sells the product, Smartstore first for search-led, trust-driven categories. Korean online shopping reached ₩272.0 trillion in 2025, and 77.6% of it was on mobile (Ministry of Data and Statistics).

    Korean online shopping reached ₩272.0 trillion in 2025, up 4.9% on 2024, according to the Ministry of Data and Statistics (formerly Statistics Korea). That's the largest total in the current series, though growth was the slowest. For US brands selling into Korea, success is less about Amazon-style listing tactics and more about choosing the right Korean channel (Smartstore, Coupang Marketplace, Rocket Growth or Coupang Vendor) and clearing the local compliance steps. This is the 2026 playbook.

    1. Why Coupang and Naver Shopping Come First

    Korean online retail runs through two very different ecosystems. Coupang is the closest Korean analogue to Amazon Prime: it buys, stores and delivers much of what it sells through its own Rocket Delivery network, with the WOW membership on top. It reported 24.6 million Product Commerce active customers in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 8% on a year earlier.

    Naver Shopping works differently. Rather than a single store, it's a search-led marketplace that sits on top of Smartstore sellers, brand stores and outside malls. Its two strengths are integrated checkout through Naver Pay and rankings that surface inside Naver search itself. Gmarket, 11st, SSG.COM, Kakao and a long tail of category specialists share the rest of the market.

    For US brands, the practical choice is binary: pick a primary platform, then add the other once the first is stable. Go with Coupang first if fast delivery sells your category (consumables, electronics, baby goods). Go with Naver Smartstore first if buyers in your category research before they buy (cosmetics, supplements, fashion, niche tech).

    2. Naver Smartstore for Foreign Brands: Setup

    Naver Smartstore is the lower-friction entry point. Its seller sign-up includes an option for sellers registered abroad, which Naver approves after a document review. The required documents change, so check the current list in Smartstore Center before you plan around it. Once volume justifies a Korean presence, two structures work:

    • Korean branch (지점): a registered branch of the US company that can run revenue-generating business in Korea.
    • Local subsidiary (유한회사 / 주식회사): the usual choice for a long-term operation with local staff, Korean contracts and liability kept separate from the parent.

    A liaison office (연락사무소) is not a way to sell. Invest KOREA describes it as limited to non-sales work such as liaison and market research.

    Whichever structure you choose, two filings come first: business registration (사업자등록) with the National Tax Service, and an online-retailer report (통신판매업 신고) with the local district office under Article 12 of the E-Commerce Act. Opening a Korean bank account in the business's name can take time, so start it early.

    Korea's VAT rate is 10% (VAT Act, Article 30). Imported goods pay import VAT at customs on top of any duty, and a seller registered in Korea charges VAT on its domestic sales and files its own returns. Corporate income tax depends on your structure and the Korea–US tax treaty, so bring in a Korean CPA (회계사) or tax accountant (세무사) before the first filing.

    3. Coupang Marketplace, Rocket Growth and Vendor (3P vs 1P)

    Coupang offers three ways in, and their economics differ sharply:

    • Coupang Marketplace (3P): you list through the Coupang Wing seller portal, keep control of price and customer service, ship orders yourself and pay the category commission listed in Wing.
    • Rocket Growth: Coupang also calls it Fulfillment and Logistics by Coupang. Any Marketplace seller can place stock in Coupang's fulfillment network, so orders ship through the same end-to-end logistics Coupang uses for its own goods.
    • Coupang Vendor (1P): a wholesale deal. Coupang buys your inventory at a negotiated supply price, stocks it and resells it under Rocket Delivery, and the retail price is then Coupang's call.

    Our standard recommendation is to grow in three steps:

    1. Validate demand for two or three SKUs on Marketplace.
    2. Move proven SKUs into Rocket Growth once slow delivery starts costing you sales.
    3. Negotiate Vendor terms only when you have steady sell-through data to price against.

    This sequence protects margin while you learn the market, and it keeps the wholesale discount for items that have already proven themselves.

    4. Korean Listing Optimization: Title, Images, Reviews

    Korean buyers judge a listing fast, and mostly on a phone: mobile accounted for 77.6% of online shopping value in 2025. Three rules matter most:

    • Title structure: brand · product · core attribute · variant. Naver Shopping explains its search order through relevance, popularity and reliability. Reliability includes whether a product name follows Naver's naming rules and whether the listing has an abuse penalty. Name the product plainly, put the term people actually search first, and don't repeat keywords or add unrelated ones.
    • Image stack. Lead with the product in use, then specs or ingredients, size, how to use, and any KC or MFDS details. Put the key purchase facts in the images and in the text so shoppers see them without tapping "more."
    • Reviews, with disclosure. Early reviews help, but a reviewer program (체험단) must disclose the free product or payment. Since the Korea Fair Trade Commission's December 2024 guideline amendment, a text post must carry that disclosure in its title or opening, and a photo post must carry it inside the image or on the first line. A disclosure buried among hashtags or behind "more" doesn't count. Paid reviews dressed up as organic can be treated as deceptive advertising under the Fair Labeling and Advertising Act.

    5. Paid Acceleration: Naver Shopping Ads & Coupang Ads

    Korean shopping ads come in two main types. They target the same keywords but run on separate systems. Naver Shopping search ads place your products in Naver Shopping results and in the shopping area of Naver search, and you manage them through Naver's search ad platform. Coupang's sponsored ads place your listings inside Coupang search. Because Coupang handles the whole path from search to delivery, its reports show the order directly. Neither system feeds the other: Coupang ads do nothing for your Naver ranking, and Naver ads don't move Coupang's.

    Be careful with CPC and ROAS benchmarks you hear secondhand. Costs vary by category, season and how many sellers bid on the same keywords. Before you compare the two platforms, check how each one counts a conversion: which purchases it includes and over what window. Set schedules and bid changes from your own reports, not from someone else's averages.

    6. Logistics & Compliance: Cross-Border Reality

    Logistics decides whether a Korean launch becomes profitable or just busy. National parcel carriers such as CJ Logistics, Lotte Global Logistics and Hanjin, plus the 3PLs that use their networks, handle Smartstore orders. Coupang's own fulfillment network carries Vendor products and, through Rocket Growth, Marketplace stock. You can run both: a 3PL for Smartstore and Coupang's network for the SKUs you sell with Rocket.

    Customs is the step most US brands underestimate. Every imported SKU needs the correct HS code classification, which sets the duty rate. Goods that qualify as US-originating under the Korea–US Free Trade Agreement (KORUS) can enter at reduced or zero duty with proper origin documentation. Designated electrical and household products, radio equipment and children's products must carry KC (Korea Certification) before they can be legally listed. Cosmetics and health supplements follow a different route: the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety regulates them, and cosmetics need a registered Korean responsible seller (화장품책임판매업). Testing time and cost vary by category and lab, so plan certification into your timeline before you commit ad spend.

    Finally, Korean consumer law gives buyers 7 days to withdraw from most online purchases (Article 17 of the Act on Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce). The exceptions are narrow, such as goods the buyer damaged or whose value dropped through use, and Article 35 voids any terms that take the right away. Build a Korean reverse-logistics partner into the launch from day one, or returns will eat your margin. Check the return-pickup options in Wing and Smartstore Center before you set your shipping policy.

    FAQ

    Can a foreign brand sell on Naver Smartstore without a Korean company?

    Yes, Smartstore's seller sign-up includes an option for sellers registered abroad, which Naver approves after a document review. Check the current document list in Smartstore Center before you plan around it. If you set up in Korea instead, use a subsidiary or a registered branch, because a liaison office may not run revenue-generating business. Then complete business registration (사업자등록) with the National Tax Service and file an online-retailer report (통신판매업 신고) under Article 12 of the E-Commerce Act.

    Should I start on Coupang Marketplace or Coupang Vendor (Rocket)?

    Most new brands start on Coupang Marketplace (3P). You list through Coupang Wing, set your own prices, ship orders yourself and pay the category commission published in Wing. To get Coupang's fulfillment without selling wholesale, Rocket Growth lets Marketplace sellers store stock in Coupang's network. Coupang Vendor (1P) means Coupang buys your inventory at a negotiated supply price and resells it under Rocket Delivery, so consider it once you have steady sell-through data on a SKU.

    Do products need KC certification to sell online in Korea?

    Only regulated categories need it: designated electrical and household products, radio equipment and children's products must carry KC (Korea Certification) before they can be listed in Korea. Cosmetics and health supplements are not KC items. The Ministry of Food and Drug Safety regulates them, and cosmetics need a registered Korean responsible seller (화장품책임판매업). Testing time and cost vary by category and lab, so get a quote before you set a launch date.
